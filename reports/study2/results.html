01 Soft prompts and LoRA produce the lowest observed distributional scores

Every soft-prompt and LoRA configuration has a lower observed score than the best prompting configuration. The best points are soft prompt retrieved source at 0.110 and LoRA retrieved source at 0.106, against GPT-5.6 zero-shot at 0.280. Steering lies on the prompting side of the comparison.

02 Adaptation moves the triangle-rank profile toward the null

Prompted outputs have an in-edge-longest rate well below one third and, in the full table, an in-edge-shortest rate well above one third. Together those two indicators mean that the generated points more often lie outside the authentic reference cloud; the pattern is not evidence of simple mode collapse. Soft prompts and LoRA move both ranks toward one third. The wrong-topic control is the extreme case—very low on the longest rate and very high on the shortest rate—which confirms that the profile responds to semantic displacement even when platform register is authentic.

Length is part of the mechanism, but it is not a complete explanation. The best soft-prompt configuration produces posts at 1.07 times authentic length. The best LoRA configuration reaches a similar Triangle-Rank score while remaining 5.42 times longer. Steering is the longest family, with its most extreme configuration reaching 12.7 times authentic length.

03 Distribution match is purchased with source loss

Prompting retains more of the source post because it is trained only through the instruction to rewrite that post. The adaptable methods learn from unpaired target-platform posts. They improve the generated pool while losing source similarity. The lower-right region remains empty: none of the evaluated configurations combines prompting-level content preservation with adaptation-level distribution match.

04 The primary result rests on a small, variable cell base

Cell-level values vary substantially. The report therefore supports selected differences between adaptation and prompting, but it does not establish an ordering between LoRA and the soft prompt.

05 The best training variant depends on the family

The lines are not parallel. The retrieved-source variant is the best choice for LoRA and for the soft prompt but not for steering, so the variant cannot be selected once and applied to every family. The figure motivates a larger ablation; the current comparison does not settle the within-family ordering.

07 Qualitative comparison

The examples make the numerical trade-off concrete. Prompting produces coherent expository rewrites that keep the source topic. Soft-prompt outputs often resemble the short title-like form of the target pool while dropping source specifics. LoRA is also shorter than prompting, but it retains more source content than the soft prompt in some tasks. Steering frequently runs to a long templated answer.

08 Metric definitions and equations

Each equation below states the finite-sample quantity implemented by the evaluation code. This distinguishes, for example, the tie-aware Triangle-Rank indicators from a generic rank statistic and the classifier calibration gap from a target-rate maximisation objective.

Shared notation and aggregation

Within bilateral cell \(c\), \(G_c=\{g_i\}\), \(R_c=\{r_j\}\), and \(S_c=\{s_i\}\) denote the generated, authentic Reddit, and authentic LinkedIn pools. A cell-level equation suppresses subscript \(c\) and writes \(G,R,S\). \(|A|\) is the cardinality of set or multiset \(A\); \(e(x)\) is the report's Gemma-512 embedding of text \(x\).

\(\mathbf 1\{B\}\) equals one when proposition \(B\) is true and zero otherwise; \(\mathbb E\) is an explicitly identified finite average, not a model expectation. \(\sum\) is a finite sum; \(\min\) and \(\max\) select the smaller and larger real argument. For a scalar \(z\), \(|z|\) is absolute value, whereas \(|A|\) is set or multiset cardinality. \(\lVert z\rVert_2\), \(z^{\mathsf T}\), \(\cap\), and \(\cup\) denote Euclidean norm, transpose, intersection, and union. Metric-local functions—including histograms, classifiers, content-word extraction, and character counting—are defined immediately below their equations.

\[ \widehat\phi=\frac{1}{|\mathcal C_\phi|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal C_\phi}\phi_c \]

Here \(\phi_c\) is metric \(\phi\)'s value in cell \(c\), and \(\mathcal C_\phi\) is the set of finite-scored cells shared by every compared system for that metric family. Thus \(\widehat\phi\) is the displayed unweighted cell mean. The 95% interval resamples cells, not posts, so a large cell does not receive more weight than a small one.

Distribution match

Triangle-Rank Metric (symmetrised) (lower is better) Target 0.
\[\operatorname{TRM}(G,R)=Q(G,R)+Q(R,G),\qquad Q(G,R)=\sum_{k=0}^{2}\left(p_k(G,R)-\frac13\right)^2\]

Symbols and functions. For pools \(A,B\), \(p_k(A,B)\) is the frequency with which the within-\(B\) edge has rank \(k\in\{0,1,2\}\), from shortest to longest, in triangles containing one point from \(A\) and two distinct points from \(B\). Thus \(p_k(G,R)\) uses the authentic–authentic edge and \(p_k(R,G)\) uses the generated–generated edge. \(p_1=1-p_0-p_2\). \(Q\) is the directed squared deviation of this rank profile from \((1/3,1/3,1/3)\); TRM adds both directions.

Chan et al. (LREC-COLING 2024). Over every triangle with one vertex in the generated pool and two in the authentic pool, the rank taken by the authentic-authentic edge is recorded; under the null that both pools are drawn from one distribution each rank is equiprobable. The statistic is the summed squared deviation from that uniform profile, symmetrised over both directions. Zero denotes indistinguishability.

Triangle in-edge-longest rate (diagnostic) Target 0.3333.
\[p_2(G,R)=\mathbb E\!\left[\mathbf 1\{\max(a,b)\le u\}\,\mathbf 1\{u>\min(a,b)\}\right]\]

Symbols and functions. \(g\) is sampled uniformly from \(G\), and \((r_j,r_\ell)\) uniformly from unordered distinct pairs in \(R\). The edge lengths are \(a=\rho(g,r_j)\), \(b=\rho(g,r_\ell)\), and \(u=\rho(r_j,r_\ell)\). \(\rho(x,z)=1-\cos(e(x),e(z))\), where \(\cos(v,w)=v^{\mathsf T}w/(\lVert v\rVert_2\lVert w\rVert_2)\). \(\min,\max\) select the smaller and larger argument. \(\mathbb E\) averages over those triangles and \(\mathbf 1\{\cdot\}\) is the indicator function. The second indicator implements the code's tie rule: an all-edge tie belongs to \(p_0\), not \(p_2\).

Proportion of triangles in which the authentic-authentic edge is the longest of the three. One third is the value under a perfect match. Values above it are consistent with generated points concentrated inside the authentic cloud and are the under-dispersion signature. Values below it are not interpretable alone: the in-edge-shortest rate distinguishes outward displacement from over-dispersion.

Triangle in-edge-shortest rate (diagnostic) Target 0.3333.
\[p_0(G,R)=\mathbb E\!\left[\mathbf 1\{u\le\min(a,b)\}\right]\]

Symbols and functions. \(g,(r_j,r_\ell),a,b,u,\rho,\mathbb E\) and \(\mathbf 1\) are defined as for \(p_2\). The event \(u\le\min(a,b)\) assigns every shortest-edge tie to \(p_0\).

Proportion of triangles in which the authentic-authentic edge is shortest. Elevated values indicate the generated pool lies outside the authentic cloud, which is an error of location rather than of spread.

Gemma-512 embedding Fréchet distance (lower is better) Target 0.
\[D_F(G,R)=\lVert\mu_G-\mu_R\rVert_2^2+\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\Sigma_G+\Sigma_R-2(\Sigma_G\Sigma_R)^{1/2}\right)\]

Symbols and functions. For pool \(A\), \(\mu_A\) is the mean of \(\{e(x):x\in A\}\) and \(\Sigma_A\) is its sample covariance plus \(10^{-6}I\), with \(I\) the embedding-space identity matrix. \(\lVert\cdot\rVert_2\) is the Euclidean norm, \(\operatorname{tr}\) the matrix trace, and \((\Sigma_G\Sigma_R)^{1/2}\) denotes the matrix square-root term whose trace is computed from the product's eigenvalues.

Fréchet distance between Gaussians fitted to the generated and authentic pools in the embedding space. The kernel-free companion to the Triangle-Rank Metric; sensitive to both mean and covariance, and to neither robustly at these pool sizes.

Gemma-512 centroid cosine distance (lower is better) Target 0.
\[D_{\mathrm{cent}}(G,R)=1-\frac{\bar e_G^{\mathsf T}\bar e_R}{\lVert\bar e_G\rVert_2\lVert\bar e_R\rVert_2},\qquad \bar e_G=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}e(g)\]

Symbols and functions. \(\bar e_A=|A|^{-1}\sum_{x\in A}e(x)\) is pool \(A\)'s mean embedding. Superscript \(\mathsf T\) is transpose and \(\lVert\cdot\rVert_2\) is the Euclidean norm; their quotient is cosine similarity between the two centroids.

Cosine distance between the mean embedding of the generated pool and that of the authentic pool. Sensitive to location only; a pool with the right centre and no spread scores perfectly here, which is why it is reported beside the Triangle-Rank Metric rather than instead of it.

Gemma-512 topic Jensen–Shannon divergence (lower is better) Target 0.
\[\operatorname{JSD}(P_G,P_R)=\frac12D_{\mathrm{KL},2}(P_G\Vert M)+\frac12D_{\mathrm{KL},2}(P_R\Vert M),\qquad M=\frac12(P_G+P_R)\]

Symbols and functions. \(P_A\) is the smoothed, normalised histogram obtained by assigning every \(e(x)\), \(x\in A\), to its nearest one of 24 shared k-means centres, adding \(10^{-9}\) to each count. \(M=(P_G+P_R)/2\). For categorical distributions \(P,Q\), \(D_{\mathrm{KL},2}(P\Vert Q)=\sum_k P_k\log_2(P_k/Q_k)\), where \(k\) indexes a cluster and \(\log_2\) is the base-2 logarithm.

Jensen–Shannon divergence between the two pools' distributions over a shared k-means partition of the embedding space. The vocabulary contains twenty-four clusters, is fitted once on authentic posts, and is shared by every method.

Platform match

Lexical platform-classifier calibration gap (lower is better) Target 0.
\[\Delta_{\mathrm{cal}}=\left|\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\mathbf 1\{p_{\mathrm{lex}}(g)>0.5\}-\frac1{|R|}\sum_{r\in R}\mathbf 1\{p_{\mathrm{lex}}(r)>0.5\}\right|\]

Symbols and functions. \(p_{\mathrm{lex}}(x)\) is the target-platform probability from the train-only TF–IDF logistic classifier. \(G\) and \(R\) are the generated and authentic target pools; \(\mathbf 1\{p>0.5\}\) converts a probability to its hard target-platform decision.

Absolute difference between the rate at which a TF-IDF logistic classifier, trained on authentic train-split posts, labels the generated pool as target-platform and the rate at which it so labels the authentic target pool. The objective is to match the authentic rate, not to saturate the classifier, which is why the gap rather than the rate is the score.

Stylometric platform-classifier calibration gap (lower is better) Target 0.
\[\Delta_{\mathrm{sty}}=\left|\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\mathbf 1\{p_{\mathrm{sty}}(g)>0.5\}-\frac1{|R|}\sum_{r\in R}\mathbf 1\{p_{\mathrm{sty}}(r)>0.5\}\right|\]

Symbols and functions. \(p_{\mathrm{sty}}(x)\) is the target-platform probability from the train-only logistic classifier over 21 stylometric features. \(G,R\) and \(\mathbf 1\) have the meanings given for the lexical calibration gap.

The same quantity for a classifier restricted to twenty-one interpretable surface features, which cannot exploit topic vocabulary. Agreement between the two classifiers is evidence that a transfer is stylistic rather than lexical.

Target-platform classification rate (lexical) (diagnostic)
\[\operatorname{TargetRate}(G)=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\mathbf 1\{p_{\mathrm{lex}}(g)>0.5\}\]

Symbols and functions. \(p_{\mathrm{lex}}(g)\) is the lexical classifier's target-platform probability for generated post \(g\); \(\mathbf 1\{p_{\mathrm{lex}}(g)>0.5\}\) is one exactly when the post is classified as target-platform.

Proportion of generated posts labelled target-platform. Diagnostic; its target is the rate authentic posts achieve, reported alongside.

Writing-habit shift coverage (higher is better) Target 1.
\[\begin{aligned}\operatorname{Coverage}&=\frac1{21}\sum_{f=1}^{21}\mathbf 1\!\left\{\Delta_f\le\tau_f\right\},\\\tau_f&=\max\!\left(0.5|d_f(R,S)|,0.1\right),\qquad \Delta_f=|d_f(G,S)-d_f(R,S)|\end{aligned}\]

Symbols and functions. \(f\) indexes the 21 stylometric features. \(d_f(A,S)\) is Cohen's \(d\) between feature \(f\)'s values in pool \(A\) and the authentic source pool \(S\): \(d_f(A,S)=(\bar f_A-\bar f_S)/s_{p,f}(A,S)\), where \(\bar f_A\) is the sample mean and \(s_{p,f}\) the pooled sample standard deviation. \(\Delta_f\) is the generated-versus-authentic effect-size gap, \(\tau_f\) is its implemented tolerance, and \(\mathbf 1\) records whether it meets the implemented tolerance.

Proportion of twenty-one surface features whose authentic source-to-target shift the generated pool reproduced to within half its magnitude. A stricter criterion than moving a feature in the correct direction.

Mean per-feature Jensen–Shannon divergence (lower is better) Target 0.
\[\overline{\operatorname{JSD}}_{\mathrm{feature}}=\frac1{21}\sum_{f=1}^{21}\operatorname{JSD}\!\left(H_f(G),H_f(R)\right)\]

Symbols and functions. \(H_f(A)\) is the normalised ten-bin histogram of feature \(f\) in pool \(A\), with \(10^{-9}\) added to every count; the bin edges span \(G,R,S\) jointly. \(\operatorname{JSD}\) is the base-2 Jensen–Shannon divergence defined in the topic-JSD entry.

Mean divergence between binned generated and authentic distributions across the twenty-one surface features. Each feature uses ten bins whose edges span the generated, authentic and source pools jointly.

Mean per-feature effect-size gap (lower is better) Target 0.
\[\overline\Delta_{\mathrm{effect}}=\frac1{|F^*|}\sum_{f\in F^*}\left|d_f(G,S)-d_f(R,S)\right|\]

Symbols and functions. \(F^*\) is the subset of the 21 features for which both Cohen's-\(d\) values are finite. \(d_f\) and \(S\) are defined in the coverage entry, and the overbar denotes the arithmetic mean over \(F^*\).

Mean absolute difference between the generated and authentic Cohen's d relative to the source pool, per feature.

Content preservation and well-formedness

Gemma-512 cosine similarity to the source post (higher is better)
\[\operatorname{SourceSim}=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{e(g_i)^{\mathsf T}e(s_i)}{\lVert e(g_i)\rVert_2\lVert e(s_i)\rVert_2}\]

Symbols and functions. \(N\) is the number of non-empty generated outputs with a recorded supplied source; \(g_i\) and \(s_i\) are output \(i\) and its paired source. \(e,\mathsf T\), and \(\lVert\cdot\rVert_2\) denote the embedding, transpose, and Euclidean norm.

Cosine between a generated post and the source post it was given. The copy floor attains one by construction and the wrong-topic control sits near the corpus floor, so this row is bounded on both sides by reference columns.

Content-word retention (higher is better)
\[\operatorname{Retention}=\frac1{|I^*|}\sum_{i\in I^*}\frac{|W(s_i)\cap W(g_i)|}{|W(s_i)|}\]

Symbols and functions. \(W(x)\) is the set of unique lower-cased tokens matching \(\texttt{[A-Za-z][A-Za-z'-]+}\), longer than three characters, and absent from the implementation's stopword set. \(I^*=\{i:|W(s_i)|>0\}\) contains the paired tasks whose source has at least one such term; \(g_i,s_i\) are the generated post and supplied source.

Mean proportion of the source post's unique lower-cased, non-stopword terms longer than three characters that also occur in the output. Reported beside embedding similarity because the latter can stay high while specific terms are discarded.

Gemma-512 mean cosine to the authentic target pool (higher is better)
\[\operatorname{TargetPoolSim}=\frac1{|G||R|}\sum_{g\in G}\sum_{r\in R}\frac{e(g)^{\mathsf T}e(r)}{\lVert e(g)\rVert_2\lVert e(r)\rVert_2}\]

Symbols and functions. The two sums range over every generated–authentic pair in the cell. \(e,\mathsf T\), and \(\lVert\cdot\rVert_2\) denote the embedding, transpose, and Euclidean norm; \(|G||R|\) is the number of cross-pool pairs.

Mean cosine between a generated post and the authentic posts of its cell.

Output length ratio against authentic posts (diagnostic) Target 1.
\[\operatorname{LengthRatio}=\frac{|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}\operatorname{chars}(g)}{|R|^{-1}\sum_{r\in R}\operatorname{chars}(r)}\]

Symbols and functions. \(\operatorname{chars}(x)\) is Python's character count \(\operatorname{len}(x)\). The numerator and denominator are the mean character counts in \(G\) and \(R\).

Mean character length of the generated pool divided by that of the authentic pool in the same cell. One is the target; both directions are failures.

Distinct-2 (distinct bigram ratio) (higher is better)
\[\operatorname{Distinct}_2(G)=\frac{|\bigcup_{g\in G}B_2(g)|}{\sum_{g\in G}|B_2(g)|}\]

Symbols and functions. \(B_2(x)\) is the multiset of adjacent lower-cased tokens matching \(\texttt{[A-Za-z][A-Za-z'-]+}\) in \(x\). The union in the numerator discards duplicates; the denominator sums all bigram occurrences.

Number of distinct token bigrams divided by the total number of bigrams across the pool. Falls when a pool repeats phrasing within or across outputs. It is a ratio, so it is depressed by long outputs as well as by repetitive ones, and should be read beside the length ratio rather than alone.

Gemma-512 mean pairwise similarity within the generated pool (diagnostic)
\[\operatorname{SelfSim}(G)=\frac1{|G|(|G|-1)}\sum_{i\ne j}\frac{e(g_i)^{\mathsf T}e(g_j)}{\lVert e(g_i)\rVert_2\lVert e(g_j)\rVert_2}\]

Symbols and functions. Indices \(i,j\in\{1,\ldots,|G|\}\) identify generated posts, and the sum is over ordered pairs \(i\ne j\), matching the off-diagonal mean of the cosine-similarity matrix. \(e,\mathsf T\), and \(\lVert\cdot\rVert_2\) have their usual definitions above.

Mean cosine between distinct generated posts in a cell. Its reference is the value the authentic pool attains, reported in the oracle column; materially above it indicates outputs more alike one another than real posts are.

Near-verbatim source copy rate (lower is better) Target 0.
\[\operatorname{CopyRate}=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf 1\{J_i>0.85\}\]

Symbols and functions. \(N\) counts all attempted outputs in the cell. \(W\) is the content-word set defined for retention. \(J_i=|W(s_i)\cap W(g_i)|/|W(s_i)\cup W(g_i)|\) when the source, output, and both word sets are non-empty; \(J_i=0\) otherwise. Thus \(J_i\) is the implemented content-word Jaccard similarity with skipped cases contributing zero.

Proportion of outputs reproducing the source post near-verbatim, which is the absence of transfer. The copy floor attains one by construction.

Generation failure rate (lower is better) Target 0.
\[\operatorname{FailureRate}=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf 1\{\operatorname{ok}_i=\mathrm{false}\}\]

Symbols and functions. \(N\) is the number of attempted outputs in the cell. \(\operatorname{ok}_i\) is the Boolean success flag stored in output \(i\)'s metadata; failed attempts remain in the denominator.

Proportion of tasks on which generation failed and was recorded rather than dropped.
